2What's Happening & What to Watch
What's Happening
  • The long end broke out. The 30Y Treasury yield touched 5.32%, its highest since 2007, and the 10Y sat near 4.71%. Heavy debt issuance, sticky tariff inflation, and a recalibration around Chair Warsh are all pushing the term premium higher.
  • Chips led the selloff. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell about 5.0% as investors pulled back from this year's best-performing corner. The move looked like broad de-risking, not single-name news.
  • Indexes eased, breadth held better. The S&P 500 slipped 0.69% to 7,692, the Nasdaq fell 1.33% to 26,290, and the Dow held to a 0.22% loss at 53,343. Rate-sensitive megacap tech did the damage while the broader tape was calmer.
  • Micron carried extra weight. MU fell 7.0% on the macro rout plus a fresh Netlist ITC patent complaint filed Aug 12 over DDR5 memory. Expectations, not the AI-memory story, are what corrected.
  • Meta added a legal overhang. META dropped 4.5% as a trial over Facebook and Instagram claims drew attention on top of the ongoing capex-ROI debate.
  • Defensives in the book outperformed. GOOGL held flat, and AMZN and UBER lost less than 1%, a reminder that not all megacap tech carries the same duration and AI-hardware beta.

4Live Macro Themes
US-Iran & Other Major Geopolitical Conflicts (Oil)
Still the standing tail, quiet today on price. WTI held near $84 and Brent near $91, little changed on the session, as talks to reopen the Strait of Hormuz stayed deadlocked. Iran is demanding the US unfreeze overseas funds as a condition, and both positions have hardened. Gulf producers keep shipping through the waterway, which is capping an immediate supply shock for now. Read it as a firm but stable risk premium rather than a fresh escalation, with the tail risk that any flare-up relifts headline inflation just as the long end is already under pressure.
Fed, Interest Rates & Inflation
Today's story was made in the bond market. The 30Y yield touched 5.32%, its highest since 2007, and the 10Y sat near 4.71%, driven by record Treasury issuance, tariff-fed inflation, and a market repricing around a hawkish Warsh Fed. A near-term cut stays off the table; if anything the market still carries some odds of a September hike. Wednesday's FOMC minutes from the Jul 28 to 29 meeting are the next read on how split the committee is, and cutting into sticky inflation risks pushing the long end even higher, the opposite of relief.
Trade Wars
Quiet and unchanged today, no fresh headlines. H200 and MI325X sales to China stay case-by-case under BIS, the 25% tariff on advanced chips routed through the US remains in place, and actual shipments to China stay minimal. For the book the read-through is neutral: no new escalation, though tariffs remain part of the inflation story that is lifting yields. The structural China-supply threat to memory and foundry names persists in the background.
AI
The dominant theme today, and it was a repricing. The chip complex fell about 5.0% as the market questioned whether the AI trade moved too far too fast, with the highest-beta hardware names taking the brunt. Two forces are stacking: rising yields raise the discount rate on long-dated AI cash flows, and the financing question, how the buildout gets paid for, keeps resurfacing after last week's AI-debt flags. The names with the least visible capex-to-free-cash-flow conversion carry the most reprice risk. Nvidia's Aug 26 print is now the swing factor for whether the capex curve still compounds.
