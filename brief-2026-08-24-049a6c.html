4Live Macro Themes
US-Iran & Other Major Geopolitical Conflicts (Oil)
The tail stayed live but the price cooled. Treasury Secretary Bessent rolled out "Operation Economic Outcast," a global sanctions plan aimed at squeezing Iran, yet oil fell about 2.5% on the day as traders took profits on last week's run, leaving WTI near $84.89 and Brent near $92. Flows through the Strait of Hormuz remain below normal and eight vessel attacks are logged this month, so read today as relief rather than resolution. For an unhedged long book the setup is unchanged: the risk premium is embedded and any fresh flare-up would relift headline inflation just as consumer sentiment softens.
Fed, Interest Rates & Inflation
The hawkish setup held into a pivotal week. The 10Y eased to about 4.72% and the 30Y to about 5.24%, a small give-back after last week's rebound, but fed funds futures still price roughly one-in-three odds of a September hike after July's fractured 9-3 hold with three dissents. Preliminary Michigan sentiment fell sharply to 51.0 with one-year inflation expectations up to 4.3%, a stagflation-flavored mix. Wednesday's PCE print and Warsh's first Jackson Hole keynote as chair on Aug 28 are the two anchors; a hawkish tone would compress cut odds and relift the long end, the opposite would ease pressure on the high-multiple book.
Trade Wars
A second front opened. US-Canada talks collapsed Friday, 50% tariffs on about $20B of Canadian goods took effect Saturday, and Trump said autos, parts, and steel move to 50% next year, with Canada set to retaliate dollar-for-dollar from Sep 8 and Ontario threatening to cut electricity and critical-mineral exports. On China, H200 and MI325X sales stay case-by-case under BIS and the 25% tariff on advanced chips routed through the US remains in place. Direct book exposure to Canada is limited, but a mineral or power cutoff is a supply-chain tail for the chip sleeve, and the escalation keeps tariffs firmly in the inflation story that anchors the Fed.
AI
The complex sold off on positioning, not fundamentals. Memory led the SOX down about 3.5% as investors trimmed into Nvidia's Aug 26 print, even though Micron's 2026 HBM is already sold out at agreed prices and hyperscaler capex guides keep rising, with the four largest cloud names now pointed at roughly $700B of AI spend in 2026. Nvidia is expected near $91B of revenue and $2.08 EPS, so the swing is guidance and any comment on the durability of infrastructure spending. That print reads straight through to MU, TSM, ANET, CRDO, CLS, and IREN, which is why the book carries its highest near-term factor risk this week.
