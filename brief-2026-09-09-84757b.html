4Live Macro Themes
US-Iran & Other Major Geopolitical Conflicts (Oil)
The event escalated. A day after Iran-aligned Houthi strikes halted Saudi energy sites, the confrontation became a direct exchange: the US military said it destroyed five Iranian tankers after an attempt to strike a US Navy warship, and Iran claimed it attacked two American vessels and eight oil tankers in the Gulf, warning crews near Kuwaiti and Bahraini ports to abandon ship. Brent settled above $100 and WTI near $96, the highest for both since May, with the 400k b/d Jazan refinery among the sites hit and fresh Hormuz shipping warnings. The distinction still holds: this is a supply-side shock, so it runs through inflation and the Fed rather than through demand, and an unhedged long book has no offset. Probabilistically the base case remains a contained but hot conflict with an elevated premium, but the direct naval and tanker strikes fatten the tail of a sustained physical interruption, which is the scenario that would keep crude bid regardless of demand.
Fed, Interest Rates & Inflation
The meeting-week call is genuinely two-sided, and today did not settle it. Some desks read money markets as leaning back toward a hold after the summer data, while others see the oil surge past $100 tilting the odds toward the 25bp hike at the Sep 15-16 FOMC from the current 3.5% to 3.75%. Inflation is sticky near 3.4% headline, and Chair Warsh has warned that underlying inflation is not slowing. The oil move only strengthens the hawkish case. The last inputs arrive this week, PPI on Thursday and CPI on Friday, before the quiet period. Flagging the uncertainty plainly: this is closer to a coin flip than a settled hold, and a hot CPI on Friday would make the hike the base case, which is the opposite of what a rate-sensitive book wants.
Trade Wars
Still a slow build in the background. The January 2026 framework left a 25% Section 232 tariff on certain advanced-semiconductor imports paired with an eased licensing path for chips headed to China, a mix analysts still call contradictory. Reexports and transfers within China remain under a denial policy, and officials continue to signal further restrictions on advanced AI models and quantum components into 2026-2027. Direct book exposure is limited, but tariff risk feeds the same inflation story that keeps the Fed cautious, and a mineral or power cutoff stays a supply-chain tail for the chip sleeve. No new escalation today.
AI
Today the story shifted from spend to monetization. Meta launched Muse, its first personal AI agent, with paid $20 and $100 monthly tiers on top of a free plan, and the stock rose 6.6% as investors read it as the first credible step toward turning heavy AI capex into revenue. That is a demand-and-returns signal, not a capacity one, and it is the exact debate that has capped the megacap multiple. On the hardware side, Micron rose 2.8% as HBM4 shipments ramp and analysts lift targets, with more than $1B in HBM4 revenue already booked, while IREN gave back part of its neocloud run. The open question is unchanged: order books and now a monetization path are validated, but a firmer rate path is the tax on the trade.
