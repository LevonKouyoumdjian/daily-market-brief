4Live Macro Themes
US-Iran & Other Major Geopolitical Conflicts (Oil)
The tail kept loosening. WTI slid toward $80 and Brent to near $89, extending yesterday's sharp drop as signs of US-Iran diplomacy, prospects of reopened transport through the Strait of Hormuz, and rising OPEC supply eased supply fears. Treasury Secretary Bessent is still pressing countries that trade with Iran to wind down links, so the pressure track has not gone away, and substantial crude still moves through Hormuz, which keeps a premium embedded. But the market is pricing a lower near-term chance of a supply shock, and for an unhedged long book that is a genuine tailwind, relieving the headline-inflation channel that has kept the Fed hawkish. A single Hormuz flare-up would reopen the tail fast.
Fed, Interest Rates & Inflation
The last inflation read before Jackson Hole came in as expected and sticky. Core PCE held at 3.3% year over year and rose 0.2% on the month, with headline at 3.7%; core has now sat at 3.3% since the Iran conflict began. That keeps prices above target but does not force the Fed's hand, and commentary framed it as room for patience on hikes. The 10Y held near 4.66% and the 30Y near 5.19%. The two anchors now are Warsh's first Jackson Hole keynote as chair Friday and how he plans to lean on the long end, since he has signaled a rate hike is not his preferred inflation tool. Probabilistically, the base case stays no hike, with the tail in the tone rather than the data.
Trade Wars
The stack keeps building quietly. Combined Section 301 exposure on Chinese semiconductors is nearing 70% as officials prepare a new 7.5% overcapacity layer on top of the 50% sector rate and the 12.5% forced-labor tariff that took effect July 24, all staged ahead of the Sep 24 Trump-Xi summit. Broader new chip tariffs are still deferred to mid-2027, a sign the administration wants to cool hostilities near term. On export controls, H200 and MI325X sales stay case-by-case under BIS. Direct book exposure is limited and the tape shrugged today, but a mineral or power cutoff remains a supply-chain tail for the chip sleeve, and tariffs stay in the inflation story that anchors the Fed.
AI
Everything hinges on tonight. The complex stayed coiled into Nvidia's report, expected near $92B revenue and about $2.09 EPS with data center near $86B, up more than 50% year over year. Hyperscaler capex guides keep climbing, with the largest cloud names pointed at roughly $700B of AI spend in 2026, and ANET broke out 5.9% today on that open-Ethernet demand even before the print. The swing is guidance and any comment on the durability of that spend, which reads straight through to MU, TSM, ANET, CRDO, CLS, and IREN. That concentration is why the book carries its highest near-term factor risk tonight; a soft guide would hit six names at once, while a confident one would validate the sleeve's run.
