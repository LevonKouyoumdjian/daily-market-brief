2What's Happening & What to Watch
What's Happening
  • Warsh chose caution over clarity. In his first Jackson Hole keynote as chair, Warsh said the summer's softer inflation prints do not show that underlying trends have improved, recommitted to the 2% target, and again declined to give forward guidance. The market read it as hawkish and moved to price a September hike as the base case.
  • Yields rose and the multiple paid. The 10Y climbed to about 4.72% and the 30Y to about 5.21%. That hit the most rate-sensitive, highest-multiple parts of the tape hardest, which today meant AI hardware.
  • Marvell's margin guide soured the interconnect group. Marvell fell more than 10% on soft gross-margin guidance, dragging on the book's networking and interconnect names even though demand commentary stayed strong.
  • IREN missed. Q4 revenue of $137M came in below the roughly $157M consensus with a wider loss and a $684M net loss. AI cloud is now the majority of revenue and 2026 is "largely sold out," but the print reset expectations and the stock fell 12.5%.
  • Money rotated to megacap quality. AMZN rose 4.0% after Evercore lifted its target to $355 on AI-driven shopping and AWS momentum; GOOGL and META also gained as investors favored cash-generative names over high-beta hardware.
  • The week still closed green. Despite Friday's slip, the S&P finished up 0.5%, the Nasdaq up 0.9% and the Dow up 0.5%, the Dow's first winning week in three, carried by Thursday's Nvidia-led rally.

4Live Macro Themes
US-Iran & Other Major Geopolitical Conflicts (Oil)
The tail firmed a touch but stayed contained. WTI sat near $83 and Brent near $88 after three losing sessions, as the market weighed better supply prospects through Hormuz against fresh disruption to Russian export flows. Iran said it reached a revenue-sharing arrangement with Oman over the strait, which raised hopes that shipping disruptions could ease, but Tehran stressed the deal does not guarantee an immediate reopening. Saudi Arabia is loading more crude from Gulf terminals to route around Red Sea risk. A meaningful share of crude still moves through Hormuz, so a premium stays embedded and a single flare-up would reopen the tail. For an unhedged long book the near-term supply-shock odds look moderate, but the reopening is not yet real.
Fed, Interest Rates & Inflation
The tone turned hawkish. Warsh used his first Jackson Hole keynote to say the summer's better inflation prints do not show that underlying trends have meaningfully improved, and he recommitted to the 2% PCE target as the central focus. He again refused to offer forward guidance or a reaction function, which pushed a majority of investors to price a September hike, up from roughly one in three the prior day. The 10Y rose to about 4.72% and the 30Y to about 5.21%. The policy rate sits at 3.5% to 3.75% after his single meeting as chair. Probabilistically the September meeting is now a coin-flip-to-likely for a hike rather than a hold, and that is the swing for the book's multiple.
Trade Wars
The stack keeps building quietly. Combined Section 301 exposure on Chinese semiconductors is nearing 70% as officials prepare a 7.5% overcapacity layer on top of the 50% sector rate and the 12.5% forced-labor tariff, staged ahead of the Sep 24 Trump-Xi summit. Broader new chip tariffs stay deferred to mid-2027, a sign the administration wants to cool hostilities near term, and high-end accelerator sales remain case-by-case under BIS. Direct book exposure is limited, but tariff risk feeds the same inflation story that just turned the Fed more hawkish, and a mineral or power cutoff stays a supply-chain tail for the chip sleeve.
AI
Demand intact, sentiment fragile. One day after Nvidia's $108B guide validated the capex cycle, the AI trade gave back ground, this time on rates and a margin scare rather than demand. Marvell's soft gross-margin guide knocked the interconnect group, and the book's hardware sleeve fell with it. The underlying signal has not changed: Nvidia's outlook, MU's roughly $50B AI-memory quarter and the AWS GPU commitments still point to a strong order book. The open question is margins and multiples, not volume. With rich valuations across the sleeve, the group derates quickly when a comp stumbles or the discount rate rises, and today it did both.
