4Live Macro Themes
US-Iran & Other Major Geopolitical Conflicts (Oil)
The standing tail firmed again. Brent pushed toward $93 and is up more than 4% on the week, and WTI held near $86 for a fifth straight session of gains, after Trump said he will squeeze Iran economically with the naval blockade still in effect and no negotiations underway. The UAE suspended financial and economic transactions with Iran, and eight vessel attacks near the Strait of Hormuz are logged this month. Read it as a risk premium that keeps building, with the tail that any flare-up relifts headline inflation just as the Fed leans hawkish and the long end has given back Wednesday's relief. The link to today's consumer warning is direct: gas above $4 is the same pressure Walmart flagged.
Fed, Interest Rates & Inflation
The hawkish setup held and the technical relief reversed. Wednesday's minutes showed a fractured 9-3 July hold with three dissents for a hike, and the Treasury's larger long-dated buyback only briefly capped yields before the 10Y rebounded toward 4.70% and the 30Y toward 5.24%. Fiscal supply is the persistent driver, with the CBO lifting its 2026 deficit estimate to $2.1T, roughly $200B above February. A near-term cut stays off the table. Jackson Hole Aug 27 to 29 is the next signal, with Warsh's first keynote as chair on Aug 28 the swing factor for whether the long end calms or extends.
Trade Wars
Quiet and unchanged today, no fresh headlines. H200 and MI325X sales to China stay case-by-case under BIS, the 25% tariff on advanced chips routed through the US remains in place, and actual shipments to China stay minimal. For the book the read-through is neutral: no new escalation, though tariffs remain part of the inflation story keeping yields and the Fed hawkish. The structural China-supply threat to memory and foundry names persists in the background.
AI
The trade rotated within the complex rather than out of it. Money looked beyond GPUs toward memory, lifting Micron on sold-out 2026 HBM and firm pricing, while networking and connectivity names (ANET, CRDO) drifted lower into a crowded setup. Rising yields and the capex-financing question still pressure the least FCF-visible names, but memory's supply-tightness story is the cleaner near-term signal. Nvidia's Aug 26 print is the swing factor for whether the capex curve still compounds across the book, and would read straight through to MU, TSM, ANET, CRDO, CLS, and IREN.
