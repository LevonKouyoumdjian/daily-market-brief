Market Brief · Friday, September 4
A hot jobs report flipped the script. Payrolls came in at triple the estimate, yields jumped, and money markets now price better than even odds of a September hike. The book split, with the chip and neocloud sleeve strong while Tesla sank on a flat Cybercab launch.
  • Indexes slipped: S&P 500 -0.38% to 7,718.60, Nasdaq -0.29% to 26,506.99, Dow -0.51% to 53,414.25. The Russell 2000 bucked it, +0.25%
  • Jobs ran hot: nonfarm payrolls +162K versus about +53K expected, the strongest since March, unemployment steady at 4.1%, wages +0.3%. Money markets now price better than 50% odds of a September hike
  • Yields jumped: the 2Y rose to about 4.37%, its highest since January 2025; the 10Y near 4.78% and the 30Y near 5.25%. The hot print, not the level, drove the move
  • Book movers: IREN +7.3% and MU +6.1% led on the chip and neocloud bid, CRDO +3.9% followed through after Tuesday's derate, TSM +2.9%; TSLA -5.9% on a flat Cybercab launch and an NHTSA query, NU -2.0% giving back part of its rally
  • Oil held firm: WTI near $91.2 and Brent near $95.9 with the Strait of Hormuz still contested and US diesel at a record. Markets are closed Monday for Labor Day, then the Sep 15-16 FOMC

4Live Macro Themes
US-Iran & Other Major Geopolitical Conflicts (Oil)
The premium is holding, not fading. WTI sits near $91.2 and Brent near $95.9 with the Strait of Hormuz still contested and cross-strait crude traffic disrupted since the spring closure. The freshest signal today is downstream: US diesel prices hit a record, which pushes transport costs into a long list of goods and keeps the inflation read live. For an unhedged long book the supply-shock odds stay elevated, and the read-through runs through inflation and the Fed rather than any single holding. Probabilistically the base case is still a contained conflict with an elevated premium, but the tail of a genuine, sustained Hormuz interruption remains fatter than it was a month ago.
Fed, Interest Rates & Inflation
Today firmed the hawkish side hard. August payrolls came in at 162K against about 53K expected, unemployment held at 4.1%, and June and July were revised up, so Wednesday's soft-ADP cooling signal has largely been reversed. Yields jumped, the 2Y to about 4.37% and the 10Y near 4.78%, and money markets moved to price better than even odds of a September hike from the current 3.5% to 3.75%. Oil is still firm, so both sides of the Fed's mandate point the same way for once. The call is no longer two-sided into the Sep 15-16 FOMC: the risk has shifted toward a hike, with August CPI the last major input. Uncertainty is real, but the balance moved today.
Trade Wars
Still a slow build in the background. Combined Section 301 exposure on Chinese semiconductors sits near 70%, with officials preparing a 7.5% overcapacity layer on top of existing sector and forced-labor duties, staged ahead of the Sep 24 Trump-Xi summit in Washington. Broader new chip tariffs remain deferred to June 2027, a signal the administration wants to cool hostilities near term. Direct book exposure is limited, but tariff risk feeds the same inflation story that keeps the Fed cautious, and a mineral or power cutoff remains a supply-chain tail for the chip sleeve. No new escalation today.
AI
Demand carried the tape even as rates rose. Micron gained 6.1% on sold-out 2026 HBM that is fully contracted, and IREN rose 7.3% as its neocloud pivot keeps pulling multi-year capacity contracts, with about $4B of ARR now under contract for 2026 capacity. Credo added 3.9%, a follow-through bounce after Tuesday's 20% derate, a reminder that the demand story is intact even when the multiple is not. The open question stays the same: margins and multiples, not volume. When the discount rate is rising, as it did today, valuation is the fragile part of the trade, and the highest-multiple names in the sleeve are the ones with the least cushion.
