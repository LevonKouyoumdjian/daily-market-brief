4Live Macro Themes
US-Iran & Other Major Geopolitical Conflicts (Oil)
The tail reopened. US Central Command struck Iranian rocket launchers preparing to mine the Strait of Hormuz on Sunday, ending weeks of relative calm, and oil jumped in response, with WTI up about 5% to roughly $86 and Brent near $88.5. Shipping through the strait remains restricted and the diplomatic track has stalled. Trump publicly demanded that Iran surrender and warned he would strike Oman if it interfered, while an Iranian official said Tehran would escalate in and beyond Hormuz if an interim deal is not fully implemented within weeks. A large share of global crude still transits the strait, so the supply premium is back in the price and a single further flare-up would extend it. For an unhedged long book the near-term supply-shock odds have moved from low back to moderate, and the read-through runs through inflation and the Fed rather than any single holding.
Fed, Interest Rates & Inflation
Hawkish, and now with an oil push. Rising crude reinforced Warsh's Jackson Hole message that the summer's better inflation prints do not show underlying trends have "meaningfully improved." CME FedWatch odds for a September hike jumped to about 66% on Monday, nearly double the pre-Warsh read, though fed funds futures and prediction markets sit closer to a coin flip, so the call is live but not settled. The 10Y rose to about 4.76% and the 30Y to about 5.25%, with the policy rate at 3.5% to 3.75%. The August jobs report Friday and ISM Tuesday are the last major inputs before the Sep 15-16 FOMC. Probabilistically September now leans toward a hike rather than a hold, and that repricing is the swing for the book's multiple.
Trade Wars
The stack keeps building quietly. Combined Section 301 exposure on Chinese semiconductors is near 70% as officials prepare a 7.5% overcapacity layer on top of the existing sector and forced-labor duties, staged ahead of the Sep 24 Trump-Xi summit in Washington. Broader new chip tariffs stay deferred to 2027, a sign the administration wants to cool hostilities near term, and the Board of Trade relief mechanism may go live before the summit. Direct book exposure is limited, but tariff risk feeds the same inflation story that just turned the Fed more hawkish, and a mineral or power cutoff stays a supply-chain tail for the chip sleeve.
AI
Demand intact, and today it led. With rates and oil pressuring the broad tape, chips and memory still rose: MU gained 5.3% on sold-out 2026 HBM capacity and a string of $1,000-plus targets, and ANET rose 4.0% on AI-networking strength and the Google partnership ramp. Nvidia's recent $108B guide, Micron's roughly $50B AI-memory quarter and the AWS GPU commitments keep the order book strong. The open question is still margins and multiples, not volume: the sleeve carries rich valuations and derates fast when a comp stumbles or the discount rate rises, as the Marvell scare showed on Friday. Today the demand signal won.
