What's Happening
  • The consumer cooled. July retail sales fell 0.6% m/m, the sharpest drop since May 2025 and well below the +0.1% expected. Sales were still up 5.0% y/y, but that is a clear deceleration from June's 6.7%.
  • Sentiment slid too. Prelim University of Michigan consumer sentiment dropped to 51.0 from 55.2. Two soft consumer reads in one morning took the wind out of Thursday's record.
  • Tech led the pullback. The S&P eased 0.17% to 7,786, Nasdaq 0.28% to 26,729, Dow 0.20% to 53,732. Broadcom fell about 6% after BofA flagged up to $370B of potential AI-financing debt, dragging the chip and AI-networking names.

  • Oil firmed. WTI climbed back toward $83 and Brent near $87 as Strait of Hormuz talks stayed deadlocked and Washington threatened more pressure on Iran. That reversed part of Thursday's relief.
  • The hike tail shrank. Soft data trimmed the market-implied odds of a September Fed hike to about 29% from 34%. A near-term cut is still off the table under Chair Warsh; hold is the base case.

4Live Macro Themes
US-Iran & Other Major Geopolitical Conflicts (Oil)
Still the dominant macro tail, and today the premium rebuilt. WTI climbed back toward $83 and Brent near $87, up about 5% on the week, as talks to reopen the Strait of Hormuz stayed deadlocked. Treasury Secretary Bessent said Washington would impose unprecedented economic measures while keeping a naval blockade on Iranian ports, with more announcements expected next week. Crude is still moving, with the US claiming up to 9 million barrels a day still transits the waterway, some with transponders off. Read today as the shipping-risk premium coming back on rather than the conflict resolving, and a headwind returning to headline inflation just as the rate debate tightens.
Fed, Interest Rates & Inflation
The soft-data run continued. July retail sales fell 0.6% and UMich sentiment dropped to 51.0, following this week's cool CPI and flat PPI. That trimmed the market-implied odds of a September Fed hike to about 29% from 34%, but this is a hawkish Warsh Fed, so hold stays the base case and a near-term cut is not on the table. The 10Y actually ticked up to about 4.68% on the Middle East premium, a reminder that oil, not just growth, is setting the long end right now.
Trade Wars
Quiet and unchanged. H200 and MI325X sales to China stay case-by-case under BIS, with the 25% tariff on advanced chips routed through the US still in place, and actual H200 shipments to China remain minimal. For the book the read-through is neutral today: no fresh escalation, and the mandatory-routing friction keeps near-term pressure off memory names like MU while the structural China-supply threat persists.
AI
Two-sided today. On demand, Micron led the book as HBM3E and HBM4 stay booked through 2027 into 2028, with KeyBanc modeling DRAM up 15% to 20% and NAND up 30% to 40% next quarter, lifting MU 2.3%. On the funding side, Broadcom's roughly 6% drop on BofA's flag of up to $370B in potential AI-financing debt reminded the market that how the buildout gets paid for is the open question. The reprice risk sits with the names whose capex-to-FCF conversion is least visible, and the high-beta hardware cohort took the hit today.
