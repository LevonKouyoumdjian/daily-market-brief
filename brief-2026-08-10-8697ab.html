4Live Macro Themes
US-Iran & Other Major Geopolitical Conflicts (Oil)
Back to the front burner. WTI jumped about 5.3% to roughly $82.30 and Brent topped $86 as US-Iran talks on reopening the Strait of Hormuz stalled. Iran is demanding the US lift its naval blockade, withdraw regional forces, unfreeze assets, and pay war-damage compensation, terms unlikely to be met quickly. Over the weekend Houthi drones struck Aramco's Jizan refinery (400,000 b/d), the first Saudi refinery hit in years, adding a supply-disruption bid on top of the Hormuz premium. Aramco says no material operational impact, but the tail is live and rising. A sustained move higher would lift headline inflation and complicate any pivot to cuts, a direct headwind for an unhedged long-duration book.
Fed, Interest Rates & Inflation
The two-sided setup got sharper. Friday's payroll drop of 23,000 and 103,000 of downward revisions argued for cuts, but today's oil spike argues the other way, and both land right before Wednesday's CPI. Yields backed up modestly, 10Y to about 4.70% and 30Y to 5.24%, as the market priced a touch more inflation risk. CPI is now the clear tiebreaker: a soft core print keeps the cut narrative alive, while any upside surprise, especially with oil re-accelerating, pushes the pause case back into view. PPI follows Thursday.
Trade Wars
The China memory thread got more concrete. CXMT raised about $8.6B in a July 27 Shanghai listing and is planning DRAM expansion, and Apple is testing CXMT memory for China-market devices pending US clearance. That is the slow-burn MU risk made visible: commodity DRAM share loss in China even as HBM stays defensible. The broader backdrop is unchanged, with H200 licensing case-by-case and Commerce still floating tariffs on foreign chipmakers that do not build in the US.
AI
A risk-off day tested the high-beta cohort. IREN, IMSR, and CRDO gave back Friday's gains on no news as macro turned, a reminder these names trade the tape both ways. Underlying demand signals have not changed: IREN's raised $4B+ ARR target, CRDO's 80%+ FY27 growth outlook, and HBM sold out through 2026 all stand. The open questions remain funding versus returns (GOOGL's negative FCF, CLS's recent raise) and whether new China memory supply eventually loosens the tightness that supports pricing.
