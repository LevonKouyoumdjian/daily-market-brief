4Live Macro Themes
US-Iran & Other Major Geopolitical Conflicts (Oil)
The tail kept loosening. WTI slid toward $81 and Brent to near $87, both off about 1%, after Washington's latest steps to pressure Iran came in less aggressive than markets had braced for, stopping short of secondary sanctions on Iran's trading partners. Traders are also watching Iran-Oman talks over a possible arrangement to restore shipping through the Strait of Hormuz. Substantial crude still moves through Hormuz, so a premium stays embedded, and the pressure track has not gone away. But the market is pricing a lower near-term chance of a supply shock, and for an unhedged long book that relieves the headline-inflation channel that has kept the Fed hawkish. A single Hormuz flare-up would reopen the tail fast.
Fed, Interest Rates & Inflation
The focus shifts from data to tone. Yesterday's core PCE held at 3.3% year over year, in line but sticky, and Jackson Hole opened today with Warsh's first keynote as chair set for Friday at 10:00 AM ET. September hike odds sit near one in three, caught between sticky inflation and a weak July payrolls print that fell 23,000. The 10Y held near 4.67% and the 30Y near 5.19%. The swing is whether Warsh leans into the hawkish dissent from three regional presidents or reaffirms patience; he has signaled a hike is not his preferred inflation tool. Probabilistically, the base case stays no hike near term, with the risk in the message rather than the numbers.
Trade Wars
The stack keeps building quietly. Combined Section 301 exposure on Chinese semiconductors is nearing 70% as officials prepare a new 7.5% overcapacity layer on top of the 50% sector rate and the 12.5% forced-labor tariff, all staged ahead of the Sep 24 Trump-Xi summit. Broader new chip tariffs stay deferred to mid-2027, a sign the administration wants to cool hostilities near term. On export controls, high-end accelerator sales stay case-by-case under BIS. Direct book exposure is limited and the tape looked past it again today, but a mineral or power cutoff remains a supply-chain tail for the chip sleeve, and tariffs stay in the inflation story that anchors the Fed.
AI
The cycle got a fresh green light. Nvidia's $108B current-quarter guide, above the $104B consensus, plus data center revenue of $89B up 117% and an FY28 growth outlook near 70%, validated the spend that had been in doubt. AWS piled on, committing to 2M more GPUs across 2027 to 2028 and adding Vera CPUs. That reads straight through to the book's hardware sleeve, and CRDO, CLS and TSM led today on it, while MU guides toward a roughly $50B revenue quarter on AI memory demand. The open question is durability of the capex rather than its near-term level; for now the guide reduces the concentration risk that had the sleeve coiled, though a rich multiple leaves less room if the pace ever slows.
