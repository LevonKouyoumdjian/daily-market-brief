4Live Macro Themes
US-Iran & Other Major Geopolitical Conflicts (Oil)
The tail widened over the long weekend. US strikes hit the Iranian mainland, including IRGC-tied sites near Bandar Abbas and Chabahar, and Iran retaliated on US bases in Jordan, ending the brief calm. Two crude tankers, one run by Saudi Arabia's Bahri and one by South Korea's Sinokor, were struck by projectiles exiting the Strait of Hormuz Monday night, with no casualties but a clear signal that shipping through the strait is again at risk. WTI rose about 5.7% to roughly $90.7 and Brent to about $95. A large share of global crude still transits Hormuz, so the supply premium is firmly back in the price and a further flare-up would extend it. For an unhedged long book the near-term supply-shock odds have moved from moderate to elevated, and the read-through runs through inflation and the Fed rather than any single holding.
Fed, Interest Rates & Inflation
Still hawkish, and oil is doing the work. Today's data actually softened: ISM Manufacturing missed at 54.6 versus 55.2 and JOLTS openings came in light, which in a normal week would cool the hike case. Rising crude offset it by keeping underlying inflation worries elevated, and CME FedWatch odds for a September hike held near 65% to 68%, more than double the pre-Warsh read, though futures still sit closer to a coin flip so the call is live but not settled. The 10Y rose to about 4.80% and the 30Y to about 5.27%, with the policy rate at 3.5% to 3.75%. ADP Wednesday and the August jobs report Friday are the last major inputs before the Sep 15-16 FOMC. Probabilistically September still leans toward a hike, and that repricing is the swing for the book's multiple.
Trade Wars
The stack keeps building quietly. Combined Section 301 exposure on Chinese semiconductors is near 70% as officials prepare a 7.5% overcapacity layer on top of the existing sector and forced-labor duties, staged ahead of the Sep 24 Trump-Xi summit in Washington. The White House also added a 15% tariff and price floors on polysilicon in August. Broader new chip tariffs stay deferred to June 2027, a sign the administration wants to cool hostilities near term. Direct book exposure is limited, but tariff risk feeds the same inflation story that just turned the Fed more hawkish, and a mineral or power cutoff stays a supply-chain tail for the chip sleeve.
AI
Demand intact, but today the multiple lost. With oil and yields rising, the richest-multiple part of the book took the hit: CRDO fell 8.7% into tonight's print, and ANET, MU and CLS each slid 2% to 3% on profit-taking rather than any estimate cut. The order book still looks strong, with Micron's sold-out 2026 HBM capacity, Arista's AI-networking ramp and the AWS GPU commitments underpinning volume. The open question remains margins and multiples, not demand: the sleeve derates fast when the discount rate rises, as today showed. Credo's report after the close is the near-term test of whether the fundamental story is still outrunning the valuation.
