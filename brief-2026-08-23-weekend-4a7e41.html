4Live Macro Themes
US-Iran & Other Major Geopolitical Conflicts (Oil)
The standing tail turned two-sided over the weekend. Brent held near $94 and rose about 6% on the week, and WTI stayed near $87, so the premium is still there. The change is on the diplomacy: Iran's president Pezeshkian said Friday he wants the war to end, framing the US memorandum as a "victory" for Tehran and saying it is "better to end the war today." Read it probabilistically. A genuine off-ramp would relieve the oil-and-inflation pressure that has fed higher yields and the gas-price squeeze on the low-income consumer, while a breakdown re-spikes crude and reloads that same tail. After weeks of one-way premium build, the risk is now balanced rather than only rising.
Fed, Interest Rates & Inflation
The hike tail eased but the long end did not. Markets now price roughly one-in-three odds of a September hike, down more than 20 points on the week after softer retail sales and weaker jobs data, yet the 10Y still rose to about 4.74% and the 30Y to about 5.28% on Friday as fiscal supply kept the term premium elevated. Jackson Hole Aug 27 to 29 is the next signal, with Warsh's first keynote as chair on Aug 28 at 10 AM ET, 19 days before the Sept 16 FOMC. His no-dot-plot, short-guidance style means the market will read the framing rather than a forecast, which raises the odds of a sharp reaction either way.
Trade Wars
Quiet and unchanged over the weekend, no fresh headlines. H200 sales to China stay case-by-case under a licensing framework, the 25% Section 232 tariff on advanced chips remains in place, and the Section 301 tariff on China-origin semiconductors sits at 0% until it steps up in June 2027. For the book the read-through is neutral: no new escalation, though the year of policy churn keeps pushing Chinese hyperscalers toward domestic Huawei alternatives, a slow structural share risk to the memory and foundry names rather than a near-term one.
AI
The whole complex now points at one print. Nvidia reports Aug 26 with consensus near $92B revenue and about 96% year-over-year growth on the Blackwell ramp, and the guide is the swing factor for whether the capex curve still compounds across the book. Hyperscaler 2026 capex is seen above $200B and industry AI-infrastructure spend near $487B, which frames the demand side, but the richest, least FCF-visible names carry the most sensitivity if the guide disappoints. Micron went into the print with 2026 HBM sold out, IREN with the Microsoft Horizon buildout, so the read-through runs straight to MU, TSM, ANET, CRDO, CLS, and IREN.
