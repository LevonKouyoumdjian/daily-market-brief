2What's Happening & What to Watch
What's Happening
  • Jobs came in hot. August payrolls rose 162K against a 53K estimate, the strongest since March, with June and July revised up and unemployment steady at 4.1%. The summer slowdown scare faded, and the print flipped the rate debate back toward a hike.
  • Stocks slipped, breadth held. The S&P fell 0.31%, the Nasdaq 0.28% and the Dow 0.49%, while the Russell 2000 edged up 0.23%. The move was orderly, not a break, as firmer hike odds weighed on the longer-duration names.
  • Yields did not confirm the fear. The 10Y eased to about 4.77% and the 30Y to 5.23%, both a touch below this week's multiyear highs. That divergence, hot jobs but calmer yields, is worth watching into CPI.
  • Memory and chips led the book. Micron cleared $1,000, up 4.8% on sold-out HBM, IREN gained 4.9%, Credo recovered 3.6% and TSMC added 2.5%. The AI-hardware sleeve was the day's strength.
  • Google won its ad-tech case. A federal judge rejected the DOJ push to force a sale of Google's ad exchange, a clear win, though the stock still slipped 1.2% with the rate-driven megacap pullback.
  • Tesla's Cybercab disappointed. TSLA dropped about 6.5% after the launch drew an NHTSA probe and analyst criticism. It is a tracked name, and the softer autonomy timeline reads as a mild positive for Uber.

4Live Macro Themes
US-Iran & Other Major Geopolitical Conflicts (Oil)
The premium is holding rather than fading. Fresh US strikes on Iran this week ended roughly a month of relative calm, and oil kept most of its gain: WTI sits near $90.6 and Brent near $95.2. Hormuz shipping thinned to six vessels Wednesday, well below the ten-day average, so the market is pricing a real, if uncertain, disruption risk rather than a one-day headline. For an unhedged long book the supply-shock odds stay elevated, and the read-through runs through inflation and the Fed rather than any single holding. Probabilistically, the base case is a contained conflict with an elevated premium, but the tail of a genuine Hormuz interruption stays fatter than it was in midsummer.
Fed, Interest Rates & Inflation
The hike is live again. August payrolls at 162K, three times consensus, with upward revisions and steady 4.1% unemployment, took the summer-slowdown case off the table and pushed fed funds futures back toward a coin flip, roughly 57% for a 25 bp hike at the Sep 15-16 FOMC. Chair Warsh's hawkish Jackson Hole tone and oil near $90 keep inflation front of mind, with PCE still running in the low-to-mid 3s. The one crosscurrent today was the bond market: the 10Y eased to about 4.77% and the 30Y to 5.23% even on a strong print, both just below this week's highs. August CPI on Sep 11 is the last major input before the decision, and the call is genuinely two-sided.
Trade Wars
Still a slow build in the background. Combined Section 301 exposure on Chinese semiconductors sits near 70%, with officials preparing a 7.5% overcapacity layer on top of existing sector and forced-labor duties, staged ahead of the Sep 24 Trump-Xi summit in Washington. Broader new chip tariffs remain deferred to June 2027, a signal the administration wants to cool hostilities near term. Direct book exposure is limited, but tariff risk feeds the same inflation story that keeps the Fed cautious, and a mineral or power cutoff remains a supply-chain tail for the chip sleeve. No new escalation today.
AI
Demand is still the strong part of the story, and today it led the book. Micron cleared $1,000 on 2026 HBM that is fully sold out and booked through 2027, with fiscal Q4 due Sep 30, while IREN rose on near-term compute that is effectively sold out and expanding per-megawatt pricing, and Credo recovered from Monday's derate. TSMC firmed with the sector. The order book underpins volume across the sleeve. The open question stays margins and multiples, not demand: with a live-hike Fed, the discount rate is the risk to rich AI valuations, as Credo's post-beat selloff showed this week. For now the sleeve is the book's engine.
