4Live Macro Themes
US-Iran & Other Major Geopolitical Conflicts (Oil)
Still the dominant macro risk, but it eased today. WTI settled around $81.25 and Brent near $87.07, both off more than 2%, as optimism firmed that a deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz is within reach even as talks stay deadlocked. The IEA still flags a supply shortfall of about 1.8 million barrels a day this quarter while the conflict runs, and its base case has Brent averaging near $85 in Q3 before easing toward $78 by Q4 as Hormuz traffic recovers. The tail stays live, so today reads as a standoff and shipping-risk premium coming off rather than the risk resolving. A relief here removes a headwind from headline inflation just as the rate-cut debate tightens.
Fed, Interest Rates & Inflation
Two cool prints in two days. July PPI was flat m/m against the +0.2% expected and slowed to 4.7% y/y from 5.5%, following Wednesday's in-line CPI. That builds the disinflation case, but the mix is not uniformly soft: goods fell 0.7% while core services firmed, and traders still lean toward a September hold near 60% on CME and Kalshi, with the first cut pushed toward Q4. Chair Warsh's hawkish lean keeps the bar for a near-term cut high. Friday's retail sales and UMich inflation expectations are the next reads.
Trade Wars
The backdrop was quiet and unchanged. H200 and MI325X sales to China stay case-by-case under BIS, with the 25% tariff on advanced chips routed through the US still in place; actual H200 shipments to China remain minimal despite roughly $10B in approved licenses. Bernstein pegs Nvidia's China AI-chip share near 8% against Huawei around 50%. For the book, the read-through is neutral today: no fresh escalation, and the mandatory-routing friction keeps near-term pressure off names like MU while the structural China-supply threat persists.
AI
The demand signal stayed firm. Memory led the book as Counterpoint flagged AI inference demand tightening supply and pushing industry revenue to records, lifting MU 4.2%. On the funding side, META recovered 2.8% back toward $600 as the market weighed its advertising cash flow against $130B to $145B of 2026 capex, and it shipped Muse Glimmer, a smaller model that runs on a single-GPU PC. The open question is unchanged: the buildout is accelerating, and the reprice risk sits with the hyperscalers whose capex-to-FCF conversion is least visible. The high-beta hardware cohort took a breather today after a sharp run.
