4Live Macro Themes
US-Iran & Other Major Geopolitical Conflicts (Oil)
The tail eased hard today. WTI fell about 8% to near $81 and Brent to near $86 as signs of diplomacy emerged, including a one-day visit by Pakistan's army chief to Tehran reportedly carrying a proposal for sanctions relief under an existing memorandum. That relief came even as Treasury Secretary Bessent kept up the pressure, giving countries trading with Iran a deadline to wind down links, with China not exempt. Substantial crude still moves through Hormuz, so the premium is not gone, but the market is pricing a lower near-term chance of a supply shock. For an unhedged long book this is a genuine tailwind: a softer oil path relieves the headline-inflation channel that has been keeping the Fed hawkish, though a single Hormuz flare-up would reopen the tail.
Fed, Interest Rates & Inflation
The long end eased into a pivotal week. The 10Y slipped to about 4.64% and the 30Y to about 5.17%, helped by a CNBC report that the Treasury could tap its roughly $1T General Account to fund larger bond buybacks aimed at taming long-dated yields. Consumer Confidence fell to 89.4, a seven-month low, with the Expectations index down to 68.2, a soft demand signal that argues against a hike even as core inflation stays sticky. Wednesday's PCE and Warsh's first Jackson Hole keynote as chair on Aug 28 are the two anchors. Warsh has signaled a rate hike is not his preferred inflation tool, so the read the market wants is how he plans to lean on the long end instead.
Trade Wars
The escalation stayed live but the market shrugged. The 50% tariffs on about $20B of Canadian goods remain in effect, Trump's move to 50% on autos, parts, and steel for next year still stands, and Canada is set to retaliate dollar-for-dollar from Sep 8, yet risk appetite returned today and the tape looked past it. On China, H200 and MI325X sales stay case-by-case under BIS and the 25% tariff on advanced chips routed through the US remains in place. Direct book exposure to Canada is limited, but a mineral or power cutoff is still a supply-chain tail for the chip sleeve, and tariffs stay in the inflation story that anchors the Fed.
AI
The complex reset ahead of the print. After Monday's positioning-driven selloff, memory and connectivity names rebounded with the group as Nvidia rose about 2% into its Aug 26 report, expected near $93B to $95B revenue and about $2.09 EPS with data center near $75B. Hyperscaler capex guides keep rising, with the four largest cloud names now pointed at roughly $700B of AI spend in 2026 and Q2 infrastructure spend up about 87% year over year. The swing is guidance and any comment on the durability of that spend, which reads straight through to MU, TSM, ANET, CRDO, CLS, and IREN. That concentration is why the book still carries its highest near-term factor risk this week despite today's bounce.
