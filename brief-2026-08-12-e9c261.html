4Live Macro Themes
US-Iran & Other Major Geopolitical Conflicts (Oil)
Still the dominant macro risk. WTI settled around $83.3 and Brent near $89.0 as fresh deadly attacks on vessels in the Red Sea and Gulf of Oman raised shipping-route risk and cast doubt on any near-term deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz. One market read: participants have not lost hope for a deal, but confidence is clearly eroding. There was no new direct strike on production today, so the bid is more standoff and shipping-risk premium than active supply loss, but the tail stays live. A sustained move higher would lift headline inflation just as the rate-cut debate tightens, a direct headwind for an unhedged long-duration book.
Fed, Interest Rates & Inflation
The swing print landed in line and the market took it hawkishly. July CPI rose 0.1% m/m and 3.4% y/y, with core at +0.2% and 2.5% y/y, shelter doing most of the work. That was tame but not soft, and with last week's payrolls, traders moved from a roughly even September call to leaning hold, pricing December as the more likely first cut. Core still sits above target, and Chair Warsh's hawkish lean means the bar for a near-term cut is higher. PPI Thursday is the next data point on whether the path firms further.
Trade Wars
China supply threads stayed in focus. Meta's planned acquisition of Chinese startup Manus collapsed, a reminder that cross-border AI deals still run into clearance friction. On memory, Apple's earlier push to source CXMT DRAM remains stalled and needs US clearance given CXMT's place on the Pentagon's list, which keeps near-term heat off MU's commodity-DRAM risk even as the structural threat of Chinese supply persists. The broader backdrop is unchanged, with H200 licensing case-by-case and tariffs on non-US chipmakers still floated.
AI
The demand side answered loudly. CoreWeave (+20%) and Supermicro (+15%), along with Nebius, blew past forecasts and raised guidance, and CoreWeave secured better contract pricing, evidence the buildout is still accelerating. That sent the book's high-beta cohort sharply higher (CLS, IREN, CRDO, ANET, MU). The open question shifts to the funding side: hyperscalers like GOOGL and META are being repriced on whether their own record capex converts to free cash flow, and META's ROI doubts today show the market will punish the names where the return case is least visible.
