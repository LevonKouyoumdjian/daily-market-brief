4Live Macro Themes
US-Iran & Other Major Geopolitical Conflicts (Oil)
Still the dominant macro risk. WTI held around $83 and Brent stayed above $86 as US-Iran talks on reopening the Strait of Hormuz went nowhere. Iran's line hardened again: it wants the US naval blockade lifted before it will fully reopen the strait, and Washington has not moved. There was no fresh strike today, so the bid is more standoff premium than active disruption, but the tail stays live with a refinery already hit over the prior weekend. A sustained move higher would lift headline inflation and complicate any pivot to cuts, a direct headwind for an unhedged long-duration book, and the timing right before CPI sharpens that.
Fed, Interest Rates & Inflation
Wednesday is the swing. July CPI is seen at +0.1% month over month and 3.4% year over year, down from 3.5%, with core at +0.1%. Set against Friday's weak jobs data, a soft print keeps the September cut case alive, while any upside surprise, especially with oil re-accelerating, pushes the pause case back into view. The September FOMC is priced roughly 50-50, and Chair Warsh's hawkish lean means the labor-versus-inflation balance in this print matters more than usual. PPI follows Thursday.
Trade Wars
The China memory thread cooled a touch. Apple's push to source CXMT DRAM stalled around Aug 5 after CXMT declined a price discount and the arrangement needs US clearance, given CXMT sits on the Pentagon's list of Chinese military companies. Apple and Micron are now lobbying opposite sides in Washington. That takes some near-term heat off MU's commodity-DRAM risk, though the structural threat of Chinese supply remains. The broader backdrop is unchanged, with H200 licensing case-by-case and tariffs on non-US chipmakers still floated.
AI
Alphabet's capex hike put the funding-versus-returns question front and center. Raising the 2026 guide to as much as $205B and issuing $25B of debt to pay for it is the clearest sign yet that the hyperscaler build is outrunning near-term free cash flow, and the market repriced GOOGL for it. The demand side has not cracked: the high-beta cohort (ANET, CRDO, IREN) actually rose today, and HBM stays sold out through 2026. The open question is whether the spend converts, and how much a name like META gets punished by read-across if it does not.
