4Live Macro Themes
US-Iran & Other Major Geopolitical Conflicts (Oil)
The premium is holding rather than fading. After Monday's mainland strikes and the two tanker hits exiting the Strait of Hormuz, oil kept most of its spike: Brent settled up 1% at $95.63 and WTI sits near $90.6 with the US-Iran conflict still live. A large share of global crude transits Hormuz, so the market is pricing a real, if uncertain, disruption risk rather than a one-day headline. For an unhedged long book the supply-shock odds stay elevated, and the read-through runs through inflation and the Fed rather than any single holding. Probabilistically, the base case is a contained conflict with an elevated premium, but the tail of a genuine Hormuz interruption is fatter than it was a week ago.
Fed, Interest Rates & Inflation
Two-sided now, but still leaning hawkish. ADP's soft 38K print is the first data point that argues for cooling, and it helped yields pause below their intraday high. Working against it, oil is holding its gain and the long end is at multiyear highs, so the inflation side of the ledger has not eased. The 10Y touched 4.818%, a level unseen since November 2023, before settling near 4.80%, with the 30Y near 5.27% and the policy rate at 3.5% to 3.75%. ISM Services Thursday and the August payrolls report Friday are the last major inputs before the Sep 15-16 FOMC. A soft payrolls number would take some air out of the September hike tail; a firm one, paired with oil, keeps it live. The call is genuinely uncertain into Friday.
Trade Wars
Still a slow build in the background. Combined Section 301 exposure on Chinese semiconductors sits near 70%, with officials preparing a 7.5% overcapacity layer on top of existing sector and forced-labor duties, staged ahead of the Sep 24 Trump-Xi summit in Washington. Broader new chip tariffs remain deferred to June 2027, a signal the administration wants to cool hostilities near term. Direct book exposure is limited, but tariff risk feeds the same inflation story that keeps the Fed cautious, and a mineral or power cutoff remains a supply-chain tail for the chip sleeve. No new escalation today.
AI
Demand is still the strong part of the story; valuation is the fragile part. Credo proved the point: 115% revenue growth and a reiterated FY27 optical ramp were not enough to hold a 90x multiple, and the stock derated 20% on a beat. On the other side, IREN rose 7.6% as its near-term compute capacity is effectively sold out and multi-year contract pricing keeps expanding per megawatt, and Micron added 2.4% on its sold-out 2026 HBM. The order book underpins volume across the sleeve. The open question remains margins and multiples, not demand: when the discount rate is high, even excellent prints get sold if the valuation ran ahead. Credo is the cleanest example of that dynamic in the book today.
