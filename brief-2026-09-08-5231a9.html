4Live Macro Themes
US-Iran & Other Major Geopolitical Conflicts (Oil)
Today the risk moved from premium to event. Iran-aligned Houthi militants struck Saudi energy facilities, halting operations at some sites and wounding more than 70 people, and Brent pushed above $98 with WTI near $94.5, both six-week highs. Separately, Iran said an agreement with Oman to manage Strait of Hormuz shipping is nearing completion, which markets read as Tehran extending control over the waterway. The distinction matters: this is a supply-side shock, so the effect runs through inflation and the Fed rather than through demand, and an unhedged long book has no offset. Goldman lifted its Brent and WTI forecasts by $5 and flagged shipping disruption as its most likely lower-output scenario into 2027. Probabilistically the base case is still a contained conflict with an elevated premium, but today fattened the tail of a sustained, physical supply interruption.
Fed, Interest Rates & Inflation
The hawkish tilt held into the meeting week. After last week's 162K payrolls, roughly triple the estimate, money markets still price about 60% odds of a 25bp hike at the Sep 15-16 FOMC from the current 3.5% to 3.75%. Inflation is sticky near 3.4% headline, core is tracking around 2.4%, and Chair Warsh used Jackson Hole to warn that underlying inflation is not slowing. Today's oil move only strengthens that case. The last inputs arrive this week, PPI on Thursday and CPI on Friday, before the quiet period runs to Sep 17. The call is no longer two-sided: the balance of risk points to a hold-to-hike outcome, and a hot CPI would make the hike the base case.
Trade Wars
Still a slow build in the background. The January 2026 framework left a 25% Section 232 tariff on certain advanced-semiconductor imports paired with an eased licensing path for chips headed to China, a mix analysts still call contradictory. Reexports and transfers within China remain under a denial policy, and officials continue to signal further restrictions on advanced AI models and quantum components into 2026-2027. Direct book exposure is limited, but tariff risk feeds the same inflation story that keeps the Fed cautious, and a mineral or power cutoff stays a supply-chain tail for the chip sleeve. No new escalation today.
AI
Demand held the sleeve up on a risk-off day. Celestica rose 5.6% as Raymond James reiterated Buy on a raised outlook and strong 2027 targets, and IREN added 5.0% as its neocloud pivot keeps pulling AI-cloud contracts. TSMC gained 2.4%, with its quarterly chipmaking-tool needs nearly doubling this year and management calling AI demand extremely robust. Micron eased 1.6% off its highs even though 2026 HBM is sold out, a reminder that when the discount rate is the swing factor the highest-multiple names carry the least cushion. The open question remains margins and multiples, not volume: the order books are validated, but a firmer rate path is the tax on the trade.
